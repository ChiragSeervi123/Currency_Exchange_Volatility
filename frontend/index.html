About · Chart Reader

About the Project

Chart Reader is a Probability and Statistics project: a graph reader. Upload a screenshot of any price chart (for example USD/INR, Nifty 50 or Bitcoin from TradingView); R reads the prices off the graph and applies the whole syllabus to them: descriptive statistics, probability and distributions, testing of hypothesis, and correlation and regression.

How it works

  1. You upload a chart screenshot in the Chart Upload panel of the dashboard (HTML, CSS, JavaScript).
  2. The page sends the image to the R backend, an R Plumber API running on your computer.
  3. R reads the candles and the price axis from the image (OCR with tesseract) and performs every statistical calculation on the prices it read.
  4. R returns structured JSON; the page draws tables and charts from it (Chart.js). The browser never computes statistics.

The status button (top right) shows whether the R backend is reachable. The API can also be explored at /__docs__/ while the backend is running.

Data

The data is the chart you upload. R finds the green (up) and red (down) candles, or the line of a line chart, and converts pixel positions into prices with the labels of the price axis. Each price is therefore accurate to about one pixel row of the chart; the Chart Data page shows the resolution and an overlay of the points on your image. The app never fetches live prices, so nothing it shows is a live quote.

Methods and formulas

  • Log return: rt = 100 · ln(St / St−1) %; simple return: Rt = 100 · (St / St−1 − 1) %.
  • Sample variance s² = Σ(x − x̄)² / (n − 1); skewness m3/m23/2; kurtosis m4/m2² (normal = 3).
  • Volatility = SD of the log returns per candle.
  • Probability: P(up), conditional probability, Bayes' theorem, joint and marginal distributions of consecutive candles; binomial, Poisson and normal models.
  • Tests: large-sample Z tests (mean, proportion, difference of means), small-sample t-test, F-test for variances, chi-square goodness of fit and independence, one-way ANOVA.
  • Regression: least squares with classical and HC1 (heteroskedasticity-robust) standard errors, Pearson and Spearman rank correlation, multiple regression on t and t².

Assumptions and limitations

  • Prices are read from an image: small candles and very zoomed-out charts are read less precisely, and line charts are sampled every 6 pixels rather than at their original data points.
  • Our hypothesis (up, down or close to the mean line) describes what the statistics of the chart suggest under a random-walk model. It is not a forecast guarantee or investment advice.
  • With thousands of observations, tests detect even tiny effects: always judge practical size as well as p-values.
  • A regression of price on time is descriptive only: prices are non-stationary, so its standard errors are not reliable.
